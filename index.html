P-101Centrifugal pump · Cooling water · Line 2

Safety first. Never open a cover or touch the coupling while the pump is running. Before any work: stop, lock out and tag the motor.

Operator guide

1. Before starting
  • Coupling guard is fitted. Nobody is working on the pump.
  • Pump is full of liquid and vented. Never run it dry. The seal can burn in seconds.
  • Suction valve fully open. Suction strainer clean.
  • Discharge valve closed, or slightly open if your site rule says so.
  • Oil level is mid sight glass. No leaks on the floor.
  • After any electrical work: bump the motor and check the arrow for direction of rotation.
2. Starting
  1. Press start and watch the discharge pressure rise.
  2. Open the discharge valve slowly until flow is normal.
  3. Do not leave the pump running with the discharge closed. It heats up fast.
  4. Listen: a smooth hum is normal. If it sounds like gravel, see "Fix a problem".
3. Checks while running
  • Sound: smooth hum, no knocking or gravel noise.
  • Vibration: nothing new compared with yesterday.
  • Pressure: steady and close to the normal value your supervisor wrote on the tag.
  • Seal: mechanical seal should show no more than a few drops a minute.
  • Bearings: use an infrared thermometer, not your hand. Typical limit is 80 °C. Use your site value if different.
  • Motor current: below the nameplate amps.
4. Stopping
  1. Close the discharge valve slowly.
  2. Stop the motor.
  3. For a long stop, close the suction valve too and report anything unusual.
5. Stop and call maintenance when
  • Smoke or burning smell.
  • Loud metal noise or strong shaking.
  • Seal is spraying or leaking a lot.
  • Bearing is above the limit.
  • Motor trips. Reset it once at most.

What do you see or hear?

No flow, no pressure

Likely: pump not primed, suction valve closed, air in suction, wrong rotation, blocked strainer.

Check: suction valve, liquid level, strainer. Stop the pump if it runs dry.

Gravel or marbles noise (cavitation)

Likely: low tank level, blocked strainer, suction valve not fully open, liquid too hot.

Check: open suction fully, clean strainer. Never throttle the suction valve.

Low flow or low pressure

Likely: discharge valve partly closed, air leak, worn impeller, blocked strainer.

Check: valves, strainer, suction joints.

Seal leaking

Likely: worn seal, dry running, misalignment.

Do: stop the pump safely and call maintenance.

Strong vibration

Likely: cavitation, loose bolts, misalignment, worn bearings.

Check: noise type and bolts by eye only. If it is severe, stop and call.

Motor hot or tripping

Likely: discharge too open (overload), blocked rotor, bad voltage, bearing damage.

Do: reduce flow with the discharge valve. If it trips again, call maintenance.

Bearing too hot

Likely: too little or too much oil, misalignment.

Do: check oil level, report the temperature, call maintenance.

Danger? Smoke, fire, spraying liquid or violent shaking: use the emergency stop, move away, then call.
